The Institute was founded on a simple observation: nobody is well adjusted, and the people who appear to be are simply better at filing.

We study what happens to a person when they are moved. From one country to another, from one language to the next, from the kitchen where everyone shouts to the office where nobody does. Psychology has a great deal to say about this.[^1] So does your aunt. The Institute takes both seriously and trusts neither completely.

> Adjustment is not a destination. It is a form you keep being asked to fill in again.

Our method is to look closely at ordinary things until they become strange, and at strange things until they become ordinary. A voicemail in a language you only half kept. A suitcase that was packed for two weeks, eleven years ago. The particular silence of a family that is doing fine.

We do not offer advice. Advice assumes the adviser has arrived somewhere, and the Institute has not.[^2] What we offer is company, evidence, and the occasional letter.

We believe that humour is a form of accuracy. We believe that being from two places means being fully from neither and slightly from both, like a badly forwarded email. We believe the absurd is not a break from the serious business of living but the serious business itself, seen in decent light.

The Institute has one member of staff. This is not a limitation. It is a sample size.

[^1]: Most of it in German, and most of it about somebody's mother.
[^2]: The Institute has, at the time of writing, moved four times.
