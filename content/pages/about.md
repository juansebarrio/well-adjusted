I'm Wided Rihana Khadraoui, an Algerian-American writer and researcher with a background in cultural institutions, a PhD in arts management, and my interests have increasingly wandered into psychology. I'm currently studying counselling.

I've spent much of my professional life thinking about how institutions shape the way we understand ourselves and the world around us. My work has taken me through museums, cultural policy, education, and the wider arts. Lately, I've become increasingly interested in the institutions we carry around inside us: families, cultural expectations, inherited beliefs, and the stories we tell ourselves about who we're supposed to be.

I'm Amazigh, a little bit North African, a tad American, a whole ass mother, and someone who has lived across enough countries to become very suspicious of the idea that there is one correct way to be a person.

**The Institute for Being Well Adjusted** is my attempt to bring these interests together.

It's a place to think about psychology, identity, migration, relationships, personhood, and the strange contradictions of contemporary life. I'm particularly interested in what happens when psychological ideas travel between cultures, when personal difficulties are treated as individual failures, and when the language we use to understand ourselves becomes part of the problem.

I'm not here to offer diagnoses, self-improvement programmes, or seven steps to becoming your best self. I take psychology seriously, but I'm suspicious of the commercialisation of healing and the idea that everyone is a perpetual self-improvement project. I believe in making space for people to understand themselves, without insisting that they become "better" and more productive versions of themselves. I have absolutely no interest in optimising a damn thing. I don't even own an Apple watch.

I'm interested in asking better questions, taking human contradictions seriously, and occasionally finding the whole thing kind of funny.

Eventually, I hope to bring this curiosity into my work as a counsellor, particularly with people who live between cultures, languages, and competing ideas of belonging.

For now, this is an institute of one.
